This is our London.

We’re neighbours, shopkeepers, makers and people with an idea and a friend willing to help. Some of us grew up on these streets. Some of us are still learning the bus routes.

See the London we built
One of us pulled up a chair. The rest of us made room.

By the next Saturday, they’d saved you a seat.

Picture a Saturday in the London we built together. You stopped in for a coffee. Before you’d finished your cup, you’d met the neighbour who could help with that idea you’d been sitting on. A week later, you walked in and someone called you by name.

The table filled up.

The people who came on their own started pulling up chairs for newcomers.

The shop became a meeting place.

A local maker put their first run in the window. A neighbour brought a friend in to see it.

The idea left the group chat.

One friend found a room, another made the poster, and on Friday people turned up.

Then the room went quiet.

The room closed. The shop window went dark. The people who used to say “see you next Saturday” stopped seeing each other. That’s how isolation takes hold. A place to meet disappears, and with it go the small chances to become part of someone’s week.
