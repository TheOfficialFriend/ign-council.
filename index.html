The Official Friend London

This is our London.

We’re neighbours, shopkeepers, chess players and people with an idea and a friend willing to help. Some of us grew up on these streets. Some of us are still learning the bus routes.

One of us pulled up a chair. The rest of us made room.

See the London we built

By the next Saturday, they’d saved you a seat.

Picture a Saturday in the London we built together. You came for one game of chess. By the time the board was packed away, you’d met the neighbour who could help with that idea you’d been sitting on. A week later, you walked in and someone called you by name.

The chess table filled up.

The people who came on their own started showing newcomers where the spare pieces were.

The shop became a meeting place.

A local maker put their first run in the window. A neighbour brought a friend in to see it.

The idea left the group chat.

One friend found a room, another made the poster, and on Friday people turned up.

Marketing Genius or Evil Scientist?

That’s the question on the jumper. Wear it and see who asks. Tell them about someone in London they ought to meet.

Then the room went quiet.

The room closed. The shop window went dark. The people who used to say “see you next Saturday” stopped seeing each other. That’s how isolation takes hold. A place to meet disappears, and with it go the small chances to become part of someone’s week.
